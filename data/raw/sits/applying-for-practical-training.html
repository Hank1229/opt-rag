Applying for Practical Training

Last updated: August 24, 2026

The United States allows eligible F and M students the opportunity to gain on-the-job training that supplements knowledge gained in their studies, known as practical training. To participate in practical training as an F or M student, you do not need to change your nonimmigrant status. Instead, you must work with your designated school official (DSO) and follow the steps on this page to apply for and receive proper authorization to participate in practical training.

Remember: You must have proper authorization before beginning any practical training. Additionally, remember that all F and M students who receive financial compensation of any kind must apply for a Social Security number.

Practical Training for F Students

Practical training provides F students with experience in their field of study during or upon completion of a program in the United States. As an F student, you can choose to participate in curricular practical training (CPT), optional practical training (OPT), or, if eligible, the science, technology, engineering and mathematics (STEM) OPT extension. U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services (USCIS) authorizes some forms of practical training, including OPT and STEM OPT.

Curricular Practical Training

CPT is an alternative work/study, internship, cooperative education or other type of required practicum that a sponsoring employer offers through cooperative agreements with your college or university. CPT is only available for F students when it is an integral part of an established curriculum. CPT can be part-time or full time (more than 20 hours per week). See the August 2026 Broadcast Message: Guidance for Designated School Officials regarding Curricular Practical Training for more information.

Application Steps

Follow these steps to request CPT authorization from your DSO:

  1. Follow your school’s processes to submit an official request for CPT authorization to your DSO so that the school can confirm that you are participating in an established curriculum where practical training is integral and make a determination that the training is directly related to your major area of study.
  2. If eligible, your DSO will authorize you for CPT with a specific employer in the Student and Exchange Visitor Information System (SEVIS), then issue, sign and provide you with a Form I-20, “Certificate of Eligibility for Nonimmigrant Student Status,” endorsed for CPT. You do not need to file the Form I-765, “Application for Employment Authorization,” with USCIS for CPT.
  3. You may begin work on or after the CPT start date on your Form I-20 and must maintain your nonimmigrant student status while participating in CPT.

Optional Practical Training

OPT is a form of practical training that directly relates to your program of study and can take place during your program of study (pre-completion) or after completion of your program of study (post-completion), specifically:

  • Pre-completion OPT: Any portion of OPT used before your Program End Date. Pre-completion OPT may not exceed 20 hours per week while school is in session. Be aware that pre-completion OPT impacts the amount of time you are eligible for post-completion OPT at the same degree level.
  • Post-completion OPT: Any portion of OPT used after your Program End Date. It must be at least 20 hours per week or full time.

USCIS may only authorize twelve months of OPT for each higher level of study (e.g., 12 months of OPT for a bachelor’s degree and another 12 months for a master’s degree).

Application Steps

Follow these steps to request OPT authorization from your DSO and apply for employment authorization with USCIS:

  1. Follow your school’s processes to submit an OPT request to your DSO.
  2. Your DSO confirms your eligibility to participate in OPT.
  3. If eligible, your DSO will recommend you for OPT in SEVIS, then issue, sign and provide you with a Form I-20 endorsed for OPT.

  4. You must sign the Form I-20 before properly filing the Form I-765 and all supporting documentation with USCIS, including paying the required fee. 

    Only file the Form I-765 with USCIS after your DSO recommends you for OPT in SEVIS; additionally, you must include the Form I-20 endorsed for OPT by your DSO when applying for employment authorization. 

    The timing for filing the Form I-765 depends on whether you are applying for pre-completion or post-completion OPT:

    1. Pre-completion OPT: After your DSO enters the OPT recommendation into your SEVIS record, you may file your pre-completion application with USCIS up to 90 days before you complete a full academic year, as long as the requested OPT start date is after you would complete one full academic year.
    2. Post-completion OPT: After your DSO enters the OPT recommendation into your SEVIS record, you may file your post-completion OPT application up to 90 days before you complete your degree, but no later than 60 days after you complete your degree. You also must file your post-completion OPT application within 30 days after your DSO’s OPT recommendation in SEVIS.

  5. If USCIS needs more information, you will receive a Request for Evidence (RFE), which states the information needed and the deadline for submitting it. USCIS will send the RFE to your U.S. address. You must respond by the deadline.

    Note: If you have an application pending with USCIS and need to update your address with USCIS, visit the How to Change Your Address page on USCIS.gov.

  6. USCIS then approves or denies your application. If your application is approved, USCIS will issue you an Employment Authorization Document (EAD).
  7. Once you receive your EAD and the Employment Start Date on the EAD arrives, you can begin work. When hired, you must report employer information to your DSO or through the Student and Exchange Visitor Program (SEVP) Portal, if eligible. You must also report within 10 days any changes to your name or address to your DSO or through the SEVP Portal.
Application Timeline

Remember: You may begin OPT only after USCIS approves your Form I-765, you receive your EAD and the start date on the EAD arrives. Visit the Optional Practical Training for F-1 Students page on USCIS.gov to learn more about applying for pre- or post-completion OPT.

  • One year after full-time studies begin: This is the first date that your DSO can request OPT. 
    • Exception: Your DSO can request pre-completion OPT up to 90 days prior to being enrolled for one full academic year if you will complete one academic year before your OPT begins.
  • 90 days before Program End Date: This is the earliest date that you can properly file the Form I-765 with USCIS for post-completion OPT.
    • For post-completion OPT, you must file the Form I-765 with USCIS within 30 days after your DSO enters the recommendation for OPT into your SEVIS record.
  • Program End Date: Pre-completion OPT must end no later than this date.
  • 60 days after Program End Date: This is the last date your DSO can recommend post-completion OPT and the last date you may properly file the Form I-765 with USCIS. This is also the latest possible start date for post-completion OPT. 
  • 14 months after Program End Date: This is the date by which you must complete all post-completion OPT.

STEM OPT Extension

STEM OPT is a 24-month extension of post-completion OPT for qualifying F students with a STEM degree listed on the DHS STEM Designated Degree Program List. Eligible students with a STEM degree from an accredited and SEVP-certified college or university who finish their program of study and participate in an initial period of regular post-completion OPT (often for 12 months) have the option to apply for a STEM OPT extension. Visit the Optional Practical Training Extension for STEM Students (STEM OPT) page on USCIS.gov for eligibility requirements.

Application Steps

Follow these steps to request STEM OPT authorization from your DSO and apply for employment authorization with USCIS:

  1. Work with your employer to complete the Form I-983, “Training Plan for STEM OPT Students,” and submit the completed form to your DSO. Remember that your STEM OPT employer must be enrolled in E-Verify.
  2. Your DSO must confirm your eligibility to participate in STEM OPT.
  3. If you are eligible, your DSO will recommend you for the STEM OPT extension in SEVIS and then issue, sign and provide you with a Form I-20 endorsed for STEM OPT.

  4. You must sign the Form I-20 before properly filing the Form I-765 and all supporting documentation with USCIS, including paying the required fee. 

    Only file the Form I-765 after your DSO recommends you for STEM OPT in SEVIS; additionally, you must include the Form I-20 endorsed for STEM OPT by your DSO when applying for employment authorization. Remember to sign the Form I-765 before submitting it to USCIS.

    You may file your STEM OPT application up to 90 days before the expiration of your current post-completion OPT and must file within 60 days after your DSO enters your STEM OPT recommendation in SEVIS.

  5. If USCIS needs more information, you will receive an RFE that will state what information is needed and the deadline for submitting it. USCIS will send the RFE to your U.S. address. You must respond by the deadline.

    Note: If you have an application pending with USCIS and need to update your address with USCIS, visit the How to Change Your Address page on USCIS.gov.

  6. USCIS will then approve or deny your application. If your application is approved, USCIS will issue you an EAD.

Once engaged in STEM OPT, remember the following requirements:

  • You must report any changes to your legal name, mailing address, email address, employer or employer’s address to your DSO or through the SEVP Portal within 10 days.
  • You must submit self-evaluations to your DSO 12 months after starting your STEM OPT employment and at the end of any STEM OPT employment opportunity. This concluding self-evaluation must be submitted at the end of any STEM OPT period of employment, regardless of its duration. For example, if the STEM OPT employment ends after 14 months, the concluding self-evaluation must then be submitted.
  • You must submit a new completed Form I-983 to your DSO if you change employers while authorized to participate in the STEM OPT extension. Remember, your new STEM OPT employer must be enrolled in E-Verify.
Application Timeline

Remember: You may begin STEM OPT only after USCIS approves your Form I-765, you receive your EAD and the start date on the EAD arrives.

  • Before Post-Completion OPT End Date: You may file the Form I-765 with USCIS to request a 24-month STEM OPT extension no more than 90 days before your current post-completion OPT expiration date.
  • Post-Completion OPT End Date: This is the last date that you can file the Form I-765 with USCIS for the 24-month STEM OPT extension.
  • 60 Days after the DSO Recommendation: You must file the Form I-765 no more than 60 days after your DSO enters the STEM OPT recommendation in SEVIS. 
  • Day after Post-Completion OPT End Date: This is the beginning date of your employment authorization for the 24-month STEM OPT extension if USCIS received the Form I-765 before your post-completion OPT end date, even if the Form I-765 is still pending with USCIS. If you timely filed your STEM OPT extension application and your OPT period expires while your extension application is pending, your employment authorization is automatically extended for 180 days. This automatic 180-day extension ends once USCIS adjudicates your STEM OPT extension application. If USCIS receives your Form I-765 after the end date for your post-completion OPT, the STEM OPT extension application will be denied.

Practical Training for M Students

As an M student, you are only eligible to participate in one type of paid training with an employer, referred to as practical training (PT). Your PT must directly relate to your program of study, and you may only participate after your program ends. PT allows you to gain training experience in your vocational field. 

Application Steps

Follow these steps to request PT authorization from your DSO and apply for employment authorization with USCIS:

  1. Follow your school’s processes to submit a PT request to your DSO.
  2. Your DSO confirms your eligibility to participate in PT.
  3. If eligible, your DSO will recommend you for PT in SEVIS, then issue, sign and provide you with a Form I-20 endorsed for PT.

  4. You must sign the Form I-20 before properly filing the Form I-765 and all supporting documentation with USCIS, including paying the required fee. 

    Only file the Form I-765 after your DSO recommends you for PT in SEVIS; additionally, you must include the Form I-20 endorsed for PT by your DSO when applying for employment authorization. Remember to also sign the Form I-765 before submission to USCIS.

    You must file the Form I-765 with USCIS before your Program End Date on your Form I-20, but not more than 90 days before the Program End Date.

  5. If USCIS needs more information, you will receive an RFE, which states what information is needed and the deadline for submitting it. You must respond by the deadline. 

    Note: If you have an application pending with USCIS and need to update your address, visit the How to Change Your Address page on USCIS.gov.

  6. USCIS approves or denies your application. If your application is approved, USCIS will issue you an EAD.

Once you receive your EAD and the start date on the EAD arrives, you can begin work. When you are hired, you must report employer information to your DSO or through the SEVP Portal, if eligible. 

Application Timeline

Remember: You may begin PT only after USCIS approves your Form I-765, you receive your EAD and the start date on the EAD arrives.

  • 90 days before Program End Date: This is the earliest date your DSO can recommend a request for and the earliest date that you may properly file the Form I-765 with USCIS for 
  • Day after Program End Date: This is the earliest possible requested start date for practical training.
  • 30 days after Program End Date: This is the last possible requested PT start date. 
  • Six months after Program End Date: This is the latest possible requested end date for PT if you are eligible for the maximum PT period.
